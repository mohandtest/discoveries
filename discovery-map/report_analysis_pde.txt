## Subject 10: Real and complex analysis (16 families)

**071. Koebe's circle-domain conjecture [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: every domain in the Riemann sphere is conformally equivalent to a circle domain; circle domains with conformally removable boundary are rigid (He–Schramm). *Approach* (from intro): finite approximations can fail — a limiting component may hold excess beyond the followed disk. The proof keeps the original conformal structure on exhausting compact cores, re-choosing it only on finite completions, and fixes analytic tests before limiting: small barriers collapse unmarked components, while period-one tests exclude protrusions beyond the distinguished disk D_b via Stokes-estimate boundary integrals. The engine is a boundary alternative: either a small compatible barrier separates an end, or a path law from an interior anchor with controlled mean arclength occupation exists — proved by a measure-family form of Ambrosio–Di Marino–Savaré modulus duality plus a fusion argument giving one law with square-summable hit probabilities over the possibly uncountable label set B; a four-path crossing argument, simultaneous transfer, and countable selection then exclude every non-circular limit. *Lesson*: fix analytic tests before geometric limits; fused path laws give uncountable boundary control.

**072. Brennan's conjecture [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: |φ′|^s is area-integrable for 4/3 < s < 4; sharp B_S(−2) = 1; strict B_b(−1) < 1/4 disproves Kraetzer's prediction. *Approach* (from intro): via the inverse f the range becomes −2 < t < 2/3 for |f′|^t; negative powers (small derivatives) obstruct — Koebe distortion gives exponent 2 where 1 is needed, so one must exploit how rarely tiny derivatives occur on a circle. A positive operator averages changes of base point with weights |Q_F(z)|², sampling dyadic heights; growth ρ ≤ 2 gives the estimate, while ρ > 2 yields a Koenigs-eigenmeasure law P with exact scaling E_P[|Q_F|²Φ∘T] = y^{−β}E_P[Φ]. Excess growth becomes a stationary process: escape/compactness estimates and an equivariant pairing of critical points force a sign on an expected Jacobian opposite to the scaling law — contradiction. *Lesson*: integral-means exponents are spectral data of transfer operators; a positive eigenmeasure turns hypothetical growth into an inconsistent stochastic model.

**073. The Falconer distance conjecture.** Compact sets of dimension > d/2 in R^d determine positive-measure distance sets — the exact threshold. Proof: L² spherical-averaging estimates for fractal measures at the critical exponent. Lesson: distance sets reduce to sharp L² bounds.

**074. Kakeya in three and four dimensions.** The δ-tube maximal operator maps L³(R³) → L³(S²) with O(δ^{−ε}) loss; 4D Kakeya sets have full dimension. Proof: critical-exponent induction on scales with two-ends reduction and degree-lowering. Lesson: critical maximal bounds close Kakeya by multiscale induction.

**075. The L log L Fourier-convergence conjecture.** Symmetric Fourier partial sums converge a.e. along the full sequence for every torus function in the L log L scale. Proof: Carleson–Hunt time-frequency machinery pushed below L², tile selection at the Orlicz endpoint. Lesson: time-frequency decomposition survives below L².

**076. Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials.** ±1-coefficient polynomials of length N with modulus (1+o(1))√N uniformly on the circle; merit factors tend to infinity, disproving Turyn's conjecture. Proof: sign construction with deterministic flattening of the sup norm, including real endpoints. Lesson: random signs plus corrections achieve sup-norm flatness.

**077. Fourier restriction for positively curved surfaces.** For every compact positively curved surface in R³ (boundary allowed), extension is bounded L^p → L^p for all p > 3. Proof: reductions to wave local smoothing (079) plus bilinear/decoupling arguments. Lesson: diagonal restriction past threshold follows from dispersive smoothing.

**078. The three-dimensional Bochner–Riesz conjecture [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: Bochner–Riesz multipliers bounded on L^p(R³) in the full strict range; the new core estimate is L³ for every δ > 0. *Approach* (from intro): a two-stage entropy argument. Stage one models positive masses moving along lines — aperture observations record velocity/position at resolutions ε^b, ε^a with an eccentricity-penalized weight — and bounds the score growth rate β ≤ γ by excluding isotropic extremizers (time-fiber richness + planar projections + Loomis–Whitney) and, in the anisotropic case, by trace arguments on the matched plane plus conditional projection tests, powered by the sharp Ren–Wang planar Furstenberg incidence theorem. Stage two transfers the bound to wave packets: away from the uncertainty boundary, coarse observations locate packets faithfully; at the boundary a coherent estimate leaves fine velocity information that grows per backward step but persists only ~2h — finite capacity forces β_q ≤ γ/2. The pseudoconformal change of variables maps each kernel shell into that phase class; shell summation plus Stein interpolation gives the full range. *Lesson*: entropy budgets with finite capacity at the uncertainty boundary close multiscale oscillatory conjectures; incidence geometry supplies the positive-mass engine.

**079. Local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation.** Sogge's conjecture in all dimensions n ≥ 2 with arbitrarily small loss above the sharp threshold; corollaries: Bochner–Riesz (all strict ranges), maximal/square-function bounds, interior restriction, Schrödinger smoothing, Kakeya/Nikodym — all Kakeya sets full-dimensional. Proof: induction on scales for the wave/extension operator — bilinear decoupling, polynomial refinements, entropy methods. Lesson: wave local smoothing is the master estimate; most classical open problems cascade from it.

**080. Sobolev endpoint in Carleson's Schrödinger convergence problem.** e^{itΔ}f → f a.e. for f ∈ H^{n/(2(n+1))}(R^n), n ≥ 2 — the sharp equality case, including planar H^{1/3}. Proof: sharp Schrödinger local smoothing plus refined maximal estimates at critical regularity. Lesson: convergence thresholds sit exactly where local smoothing dictates.

**081. David–Semmes Riesz-transform problem, higher codimension.** For d ≥ 4, 2 ≤ n ≤ d−2, n-Ahlfors–David regular measures with uniformly L²-bounded truncated n-Riesz transforms are uniformly n-rectifiable, dimension-only bounds. Proof: higher-codimension correlation-of-jumps/dyadic mass-conservation with a big-pieces substitute and Tb testing. Lesson: odd-kernel singular-integral bounds detect rectifiability in any codimension.

**082. Triangular Hilbert transform, maximal and variational.** Maximal and annular r-variation bounds (r > 2), L³(R²) → L^{3/2}(R²); a third-input pairing settles the symmetric L³×L³×L³ estimate. Proof: higher-order time-frequency analysis with tree selection in two-dimensional frequency geometry. Lesson: variational control of multilinear singular integrals comes with higher time-frequency complexity.

**083. Stein's conjecture for Hilbert transforms along Lipschitz directions.** The planar Hilbert transform along any Lipschitz unit vector field is uniformly strong-L² bounded at lengths ≲ 1/Lip, uniformly over inner truncations — an L² principal value. Proof: reduction to directional models and L² orthogonality/commutator estimates. Lesson: short-scale Lipschitz control beats the rough-vector-field obstruction.

**084. Erdős similarity conjecture, geometric case.** For every q ∈ (0,1), compact subsets of [0,1] of measure arbitrarily close to 1 avoid all translated dilates of {q^n}, both signs. Proof: probabilistic thinning of a fat Cantor set with a union bound over forbidden copies. Lesson: countable forbidden families are avoidable by measure-1 sets.

**085. Endpoint regularity of the planar centered maximal function.** The centered disk maximal operator maps W^{1,1}(R²) boundedly to W^{1,1} — the Hajłasz–Onninen planar centered case. Proof: gradient transport along the maximizing-ball correspondence, repairing centeredness via near-unique maximal-ball selection. Lesson: maximal-function regularity hinges on the maximizing-center map's geometry.

**086. L³ bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform.** The principal-value trilinear Hilbert transform (shifts x−t, x−2t, x−3t) maps L³(R)³ → L¹(R). Proof: multilinear time-frequency analysis with modulation-symmetry exhaustion and refined tree selection. Lesson: borderline exponent cases fall to tree-selection induction.

## Subject 11: Functional analysis (11 families)

**322. Tingley's sphere-isometry problem.** Every surjective isometry between unit spheres of real Banach spaces extends uniquely to a surjective real-linear isometry, unrestricted. Proof: Mazur–Ulam-type reconstruction from metric midpoints and supporting hyperplanes. Lesson: the sphere's metric determines the whole Banach space.

**323. Relative independence of the separable quotient problem.** Consistent (measurable cardinal) that every infinite-dimensional Banach space has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient; CH gives counterexamples over both fields — ZFC-independence. Proof: measurable-cardinal model construction; CH diagonalization for the negative side. Lesson: a classical problem sits at standard independence height.

**324. Lipschitz equivalence without linear isomorphism.** Separable real Banach spaces that are bi-Lipschitz equivalent but not linearly isomorphic — negative solution to the Lipschitz-isomorphism problem. Proof: spaces with identical nonlinear metric structure while a linear invariant differs. Lesson: bi-Lipschitz equivalence does not linearize.

**325. The complete Crouzeix conjecture [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: for every bounded operator A and every finite matrix-valued polynomial P, ||P[A]|| ≤ 2·max_{W(A)} ||P|| — sharp, all sizes, infinite dimensions; W(A) is a complete 2-spectral set. *Approach* (from intro): a structural route in three steps. (1) *Optimal metric*: for W(A) in an admissible (convex, real-analytic Jordan) domain Ω, minimize τ with I ⪯ H ⪯ τI, T*HT ⪯ H for T = f(A); the attained minimum equals the least condition number of a similarity making f(A) a contraction. (2) *Common positive representation*: with S = H^{1/2}, one positive density Λ(t) = j(t)Φ_D(ψ(t)) from the Delyon–Delyon disk-resolvent kernel represents v[A′] for every coefficient size and holomorphic v. (3) *κ ≤ 2*: extremal eigenvectors of S ⊗ I give boundary vector fields; partial traces and a transpose identification produce two positive densities compared in boundary Hilbert–Schmidt L² — positivity bounds the squared cross norm by the diagonal sum, the zero matrix mean separates terms into orthogonal Fourier subspaces, and κ vs κ^{−1} factors close κ ≤ 2. Infinite dimension follows by finite compressions. *Lesson*: numerical-range constants are metric-optimization problems — the sharp constant is a best-similarity condition number.

**326. Cotype–cotype conjecture under the approximation property.** A real Banach space with AP is K-convex iff it and its dual have finite Rademacher cotype; dual finite cotype forces nontrivial type. Proof: Rademacher/Gaussian averaging-operator iteration, AP approximants breaking the K-convexity barrier. Lesson: primal-dual finite cotype yields K-convexity under AP.

**327. Markov type characterizes superreflexivity.** Markov type p > 1 implies an equivalent uniformly convex norm; with the converse, nontrivial Markov type characterizes superreflexivity (Naor's question). Proof: a uniform convexity modulus extracted from iterated martingale-type inequalities on Markov chains. Lesson: stochastic metric invariants match linear superreflexivity.

**328. Fixed points of nonexpansive maps in reflexive spaces.** Every nonexpansive selfmap of a closed bounded convex set in a real reflexive space has a fixed point (Kirk), original norm. Proof: asymptotic-center/weak-convergence arguments removing uniform convexity via refined asymptotic minimization. Lesson: reflexivity alone suffices for metric fixed-point theory.

**329. Counterexample to Pietsch's metric-entropy duality conjecture.** No universal constants realize dimension-free duality between covering numbers of a body and its polar; origin-symmetric counterexamples exist even with a cube as covering body. Proof: explicit asymptotic-convex-geometry families with diverging duality ratios. Lesson: entropy duality has irreducible dimensional asymmetry.

**330. Kalton's Lipschitz-free approximation question [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: a countable uniformly discrete metric space M whose Lipschitz-free space F(M) has AP but fails λ-BAP for every finite λ — finite-rank operators approximate the identity on compacts, but no uniform norm bound; F(M) is also not approximable. *Approach* (from intro): blocks. For each p ≥ 1, a bounded countably infinite pointed space (M_p) with d ≥ 1, diam ≤ 300p²+150p+5, and finite anchors A_p such that every finite-rank operator of norm ≤ p errs by ≥ 1/2 at some anchor — an infinite-block obstruction (impossible for finite spaces). Each block has F(M_p) ≅ ℓ¹, so coordinate truncations give 2B_p-BAP per block; blocks are glued at a common basepoint with additive distances, and the per-p obstructions accumulate so any fixed λ fails on block p > λ. Corollary: an equivalent norm on real ℓ¹ failing MAP. *Lesson*: BAP failure is compactness-vs-boundedness — obstructions at every scale coexist with blockwise metric approximation.

**331. Asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity without AUC renorming.** A reflexive space with asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity but no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm; depth-k diamonds have distortion ≥ 1+k/12. Proof: diamond-graph tree-space construction with tuned renorming plus a diamond-distortion lower bound. Lesson: the asymptotic-convexity hierarchy does not collapse under reflexivity.

**332. Metric Markov cotype two for ℓ₁.** Real ℓ₁ has metric Markov cotype two; Lipschitz maps from Hilbert subsets into ℓ₁ extend with universal loss (Ball's problem). Proof: nonlinear Markov-chain cotype inequality directly for metric ℓ₁, bypassing linear structure. Lesson: nonlinear Markov cotype holds for linearly bad targets.

## Subject 09: Partial differential equations (16 families)

**362. Global smoothness for relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell.** Large-data global existence and uniqueness in 3D one-species Vlasov–Maxwell; smooth compactly supported data with finite-energy fields stay smooth for all time. Proof: continuation criteria via field-particle energy estimates bounding critical field norms by velocity averages. Lesson: velocity averaging rules out kinetic blowup.

**363. Boltzmann nonuniqueness with exact local conservation.** Two distinct global entropy solutions of periodic 3D hard-sphere Boltzmann from one datum, L¹-strongly continuous, exactly conserving mass, momentum, kinetic energy. Proof: convex integration of non-classical weak solutions satisfying all conservation laws. Lesson: entropy plus conservation does not select uniqueness.

**364. Kinetic limits and fluctuations over the Boltzmann lifespan.** Nonlinear Boltzmann derived from 3D grand-canonical gases with attractive-well, singular-core potentials over every regular kinetic interval; companion gives hard-sphere Gaussian fluctuations. Proof: BBGKY Duhamel-tree expansion with Gaussian-decay bookkeeping; central-limit scaling. Lesson: derivation survives attraction with pair exclusion and summable Gaussian bounds.

**365. Joint metric and connection recovery from one boundary patch.** Zero-frequency data on one boundary patch determine a smooth metric and unitary connection (rank-2, dim ≥ 3) up to diffeomorphism and patch-fixed gauge; measurable conductivities can share full-boundary data. Proof: boundary-control methods for the ray transform plus a smooth-vs-measurable dichotomy. Lesson: smoothness enables localized recovery.

**366. The planar Mumford–Shah regularity conjecture.** Reduced planar minimizers have C^{1,α} discontinuity sets: a regular crack tip or three arcs meeting at 120°, finitely many components per compact set. Proof: blow-up analysis with monotonicity formulas and tangent-configuration classification, completing De Giorgi's program. Lesson: monotonicity + tangents resolve free-discontinuity regularity in 2D.

**367. The critical dimension for the one-phase Bernoulli problem.** n = 7 is the first dimension with a nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer; free boundaries smooth through dimension 6, singular sets ≤ n−7. Proof: Simons-type program — monotonicity, calibration, minimal-cone classification. Lesson: Bernoulli boundaries obey a minimal-cone-like threshold.

**368. The three-dimensional Ball–Evans approximation problem.** Every W^{1,p} homeomorphism between bounded domains in R³ (p < ∞) is a strong W^{1,p} limit of smooth diffeomorphisms onto the same target. Proof: mollification with constraint repair preserving injectivity, 3D-specific topological control. Lesson: homeomorphic weak limits stay diffeomorphism-approximable in 3D.

**369. The hot spots conjecture for simply connected planar domains.** On smooth bounded simply connected planar domains, first positive Neumann eigenfunctions have no interior critical points; extrema lie on the boundary, multiplicity allowed. Proof: conformal transplantation plus nodal-line/critical-point analysis via univalence. Lesson: conformal geometry turns spectral questions into univalence.

**370. The subcritical Lane–Emden and Hénon–Lane–Emden conjectures.** −Δu = |x|^A v^p, −Δv = |x|^B u^q has no positive entire solution when (n+A)/(p+1) + (n+B)/(q+1) > n−2 — no symmetry assumptions; exact criterion for n ≥ 3, A,B > −2. Proof: weighted scaling identities with a symmetry-free Liouville estimate. Lesson: scaling thresholds are sharp without symmetry.

**371. Stable blowup for a defocusing Schrödinger equation.** For large odd power, an open set of H^k(T^12) (k > 8) data blows up in finite time, stably. Proof: concentration ansatz on the high-dimensional torus with modulation analysis proving orbital stability of the mechanism. Lesson: defocusing does not preclude stable blowup in high dimension.

**372. Global uniqueness in smooth isotropic elasticity.** Full static boundary displacement-to-traction data determine both smooth Lamé moduli on any bounded smooth domain in R³, given μ > 0, 3λ+2μ > 0 — no analyticity or closeness. Proof: Runge approximation with unique continuation and a positivity-driven boundary step. Lesson: convexity gives global Calderón uniqueness without analyticity.

**373. Nonattainment in three-marginal Coulomb transport.** Optimal Coulomb configurations need not be deterministic maps of the first particle, even for smooth identical densities; Monge = Kantorovich but unattained — all inverse-power Riesz costs, dim ≥ 2. Proof: duality with cost-singularity analysis; candidate maps violate first-order optimality near the singularity. Lesson: singular multi-marginal costs fail the Monge ansatz generically.

**374. Sharp one-third stability of optimal transport maps.** For uniform source on a convex body, ||T_ν−T_μ||_{L²} ≤ C W₂^{1/3} uniformly on compact target families; sharp via three-atom targets, disproving Letrouit's square root. Proof: linearized Monge–Ampère estimates plus matching three-atom lower bounds. Lesson: map stability is a cubic root of the Kantorovich distance.

**375. De Giorgi's conjecture in dimension eight.** Entire C² solutions u: R⁸ → (−1,1) of Δu = u³−u, strictly monotone in one direction, depend on one coordinate — the sharp endpoint; stable solutions on R⁷ are wells or planar transitions, no energy assumption. Proof: Savin-program endpoint extension — plane-fitting via a sliding improvement theorem. Lesson: De Giorgi holds through dimension 8; stability classifies one dimension below.

**376. Universal computation in forced Navier–Stokes flows [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: at any fixed positive computable viscosity, smooth mean-zero forces on the flat T³ make fluid starting from rest perform universal computation: a terminating compiler maps a Turing machine + input to a finite force program, and a designated particle from a fixed point enters a fixed open set exactly when the machine halts. Force smooth with bounded derivatives of every order, kinetic energy uniformly bounded, stationary after t = 1. *Approach* (from intro — the mechanism): (1) *Tape encoding*: the tape lives in positional expansions of real coordinates à la Moore's generalized shifts; local instructions become piecewise-affine maps. (2) *Invertibility obstacle*: smooth flows have invertible finite-time maps but a Turing instruction may erase — following Landauer/Bennett, a seven-row reversible recording table retains instruction history so every branch is invertible; its inverse and initialization are proved here, not imported. (3) *Planar Hamiltonian realization*: gapped tape coordinates give separated source and target rectangles; each branch is realized by area-preserving motion — ordered extraction to a parking region, reciprocal deformation, ordered delivery. (4) *Clock*: the third spatial coordinate supplies phase via a mean-zero spatial clock. (5) *Force identity*: for any prescribed divergence-free velocity U, F = U_t + (U·∇)U − νΔU makes (U, 0) the unique classical solution — the compiler prescribes U from the machine table without running the machine. (6) *From rest*: computably flat cutoffs ramp the field up by t = 1, after which the force is stationary; halting undecidability then transfers to the trajectory event. Companion papers: Velocity-Field Detection (next), Rapidly Vanishing Forcing, Scalar Potentials and Slow Clocks, Fixed-Particle Test, Incompressible Box Transport. *Lesson*: halting undecidability embeds into smooth PDE dynamics via reversible-communication primitives; any existence theory with a prescribed-velocity identity inherits computational universality.

**376b. Velocity-Field Detection of Computation in Forced Navier–Stokes Flows [DEEP DIVE].** *Finding*: an Eulerian version: on T³ the fixed event that u₃ exceeds 1/2 somewhere in a fixed strip occurs exactly when the machine halts; on R²×T a fixed integral of u₃ over expanding translation regions does likewise, with all mixed derivatives of the force globally bounded. No particle tracking. *Approach* (from intro): the vertical velocity obeys an advection–diffusion equation, so viscosity smears the encoded information; the two constructions pay differently. On T³ the horizontal mechanism stays in one compact chart and uses successively shorter stirring bursts: each burst injects a vertical pulse whose pointwise error is controlled over short times, and later tests are sped up so diffusion never erases the 1/2 margin — at the price of derivative bounds growing with time. On R²×T a moving cutoff controls the total escaped mass across the expanding array, so amplitudes and all mixed derivatives stay globally bounded, at the price of losing a time-independent compact support. The planar reversible box-transport logic supplies the computation; thresholds and regions are machine-independent. *Lesson*: computation is readable off the velocity field itself; the price of Eulerian observation is either unbounded-in-time force derivatives or an expanding domain.

**377. Interior C^{1,α} regularity for infinity-harmonic functions.** Bounded infinity-harmonic functions in R^d, d ≥ 3, are uniformly C^{1,α_d} in the interior, α_d > 0 depending only on dimension; gradient bounds on the half ball controlled by the oscillation; exponent not explicit. Proof: refined structure theory of the Aronsson equation — comparison-geometry control of the gradient's direction distribution upgrading Lipschitz to Hölder differentiability. Lesson: infinity-harmonic differentiability resolves positively in every dimension ≥ 3.

## Meta-lessons (Analysis, PDE & Functional Analysis)

- **Transfer operators, eigenmeasures, and stationary stochastic encodings.** Brennan builds a positive eigenmeasure for a dyadic base-point-change operator and derives a sign contradiction in an expected Jacobian; Koebe fuses path laws via modulus–probability duality; Bochner–Riesz budgets information across observation scales. Pattern: encode hypothetical failure as a probability law, then find its internal inconsistency.
- **Induction-on-scales with an uncertainty-boundary bottleneck.** Bochner–Riesz and wave local smoothing share one architecture: a positive (Furstenberg/incidence) estimate away from the uncertainty boundary, a coherent estimate at it, and a finite-capacity argument showing fine-scale leftover information cannot accumulate. Local smoothing (079) is the master result — restriction, Kakeya, Nikodym, Schrödinger convergence, and maximal estimates all cascade from it.
- **Universality via reversible primitives + a prescribed-solution identity.** The Navier–Stokes cluster embeds Turing machines through: positional tape coordinates → reversible recording tables (Bennett/Landauer) → area-preserving box transport → a spatial clock → the force identity F = U_t + (U·∇)U − νΔU. The compiler never runs the machine; undecidability transfers to particle entry, velocity thresholds, and integral observations, with explicit trade-offs (force boundedness vs compact support, particle vs Eulerian observation).
- **Numerical/verification content is quantitative but not computational.** Explicit constants appear in the Lipschitz-free construction (diam ≤ 300p²+150p+5, error ≥ 1/2 at norm p, distortion ≥ 1+k/12), the sharp 1/3 transport exponent, the Bernoulli dimension-7 and De Giorgi dimension-8 thresholds, and NS derivative/energy bounds. The verifiable content is combinatorial bookkeeping (entropy budgets, incidence counts, table inverses), not certified numerics.
- **Structural reformulation and counterexamples.** Crouzeix converts a spectral-set constant into a best-similarity condition number via a positivity/Fourier-orthogonality boundary comparison; transport stability is a linearized Monge–Ampère comparison. Counterexamples — Boltzmann nonuniqueness, Lipschitz-equivalent non-isomorphic spaces, AP-without-BAP free spaces, Pietsch-duality violation, Coulomb nonattainment, ZFC-independence of the separable quotient — map the landscape from both directions.
