# Report: Subject 13 (Combinatorics, 37 families) & Subject 12 (Dynamical Systems/Ergodic Theory, 12 families)

## Part I — Combinatorics

**155. Periodic tiling fails in dimension 3.** A finite Z^3 tile with no fully periodic tiling; unit-cube thickening gives an R^3 counterexample. *Approach:* substitution gadget forcing nonperiodic hierarchy. *Lesson:* finite gadgets yield aperiodicity at minimal dimension.

**156. Borsuk's conjecture fails in dimension 9.** Rank-one projectors onto lines in R^4 (Frobenius metric) form a compact R^9 set needing >10 smaller-diameter covers. *Approach:* algebraic construction, counting lower bound. *Lesson:* varieties carry finite covering obstructions.

**157. Counterexamples to Hadwiger and Colin de Verdière conjectures.** *(DEEP DIVE)* Arbitrarily large graphs with α ≤ 2 have fractional chromatic number exceeding their Hadwiger number — Hadwiger fails even fractionally (Reed–Seymour); also χ_list ≤ C·h(G) universally; the Colin de Verdière chromatic bound fails fractionally.

*Approach:* Graphs come from an F_2 "hole relation": positions are non-adjacent iff a tensor-certified hole exists (shared vector in V; identities u_j U_i = r(λ_i), r(λ)=a+T(λ,·) for a symmetric bilinear form T; a(λ_i)+a(λ_j)=1); summing around a hole triangle cancels every shared and bilinear term, leaving 0=1, so holes are triangle-free — α ≤ 2, χ ≥ m/2. A distribution theorem (entropy minimizers, containers, capacitated cuts, weak-regularity energy increments) then forces a large connected matching in holes, obstructing clique minors; Boolean moment matrices certify cross-contractions.

*Explicitness:* Fully parametric with explicit constants (C0=1000, g=10^9+1, M=2^1000) and an O(n)-bit record — deterministic, finitely describable; but the proof uses analytic limits (leaf-histogram compactness) and instance verification is NP-hard-infeasible: explicit in principle, not machine-checkable. *Lesson:* F_2 parity certificates plus probabilistic density theorems defeat conjectures supported by every structured graph class; the failure sits exactly at the chromatic extreme.

**158. Euclidean plane not five-colorable.** Every 5-coloring of the plane (no measurability assumption) has a monochromatic unit pair; Hadwiger–Nelson reduces to χ ∈ {6,7}. *Approach:* finite certificate — an explicit 5-chromatic unit-distance graph. *Lesson:* finite witnesses reduce continuum problems to finite checks.

**159. Erdős's reciprocal-sum conjecture; quasipolynomial Szemerédi.** Divergent reciprocal sum forces k-term APs for all k; k-AP-free subsets of [N] obey quasipolynomial bounds. *Approach:* density-increment transference onto logarithmic (divisor-chain) scales. *Lesson:* divergent weights substitute for density when increments are scale-conditional.

**160. Superexponential van der Waerden numbers.** W_r(k) grows superexponentially in k, uniformly in r, resolving Erdős's growth question. *Approach:* probabilistic lower bound with alteration-controlled progression counts. *Lesson:* uniform-in-parameter bounds need explicit dependence tracking.

**161. Counterexamples to Sidorenko and the forcing conjecture.** A 35-vertex, 66-edge bipartite pattern occurs less often than in random graphs of equal density, also defeating Skokan–Thoma forcing. *Approach:* graphon variational calculus — entropy minimization computes the density exactly. *Lesson:* asymmetric gadgets plus graphon limits separate density from quasirandomness.

**162. Counterexamples to Ryser and Gyárfás covering conjectures.** Intersecting (q+1)-partite, (q+1)-uniform hypergraphs with covering number q+1 for large primes q; an extension-field variant kills Gyárfás's tree-cover conjecture. *Approach:* finite-field/projective constructions forcing covers to take a full part. *Lesson:* incidence designs defeat greedy covering bounds.

**164. Hindman's finite sums and products conjecture.** Every finite coloring of N contains prescribed-size sets whose nonempty subset sums and products share one color. *Approach:* idempotent ultrafilter (central sets) machinery with a multiplicative transfer. *Lesson:* ultrafilter algebra handles interacting semigroup operations.

**165. Exact crossing numbers of complete and complete bipartite graphs.** *(DEEP DIVE)* cr(K_n) = (1/4)⌊n/2⌋⌊(n−1)/2⌋⌊(n−2)/2⌋⌊(n−3)/2⌋ for all n (Harary–Hill), and the Zarankiewicz formula for K_{m,n}, for unrestricted continuous-arc drawings.

*Approach:* Per the preprint, the lower bound is algebraic, not a drawing restriction: signed intersections (orientation retained) plus local cyclic orders feed a Tutte/Woodall-style corrected bilinear pairing vanishing on cycle pairs, and polynomial interpolation converts this to a rank bound. For n = 2s+1, a space of polynomials in four variables (two edges' endpoints), symmetric per edge with degree ≤ s−2 each, has dimension exactly Z(2s+1); the central lemma — an even-node detector built from Lagrange weights — proves evaluation at edge pairs with nonzero signed crossing count is injective. Hence crossings ≥ dim = conjectured value; classical drawings match it. *Explicitness:* exact formula, finite polynomial-identity proof, checkable in principle. *Lesson:* rank counting over signed-crossing matrices turns topological minimization into exact linear algebra.

**166. Higher-dimensional Erdős distinct distances.** *(DEEP DIVE)* For fixed d ≥ 3, every n-point set in R^d determines ≥ c_d n^{2/d} distinct distances — the optimal grid order.

*Approach:* Per the preprint: rigid-motion reduction (Elekes–Sharir/Guth–Katz lineage) via Bardwell–Evans–Sheffer to incidences of (d−1)-flats in R^{2d−2}; induction on dimension through Solymosi–Vu recurrences with the Tidor–Yu–Zakharov three-dimensional bound upgraded. The novelty is degree-sensitive concentration control: Hilbert function estimates (Chardin) and approximate complete intersections (Walsh) constrain flat concentration at *several simultaneous degree scales*, with a uniform concentration theorem for evaluation flats of skew forms proved in full. *Explicitness:* sharp-exponent analytic result, classical algebraic geometry. *Lesson:* incidence inductions succeed when concentration is controlled at multiple polynomial-degree scales at once.

**167. Pinned distances; planar unit-distance power saving.** All but o(n) points of any planar n-set see ≥ n^{1−ε} distinct distances; unit-distance pairs are O(n^{4/3−ε}). *Approach:* polynomial partitioning plus weak radial estimates; removal-type energy control. *Lesson:* pinned statements follow when the exceptional set has a paid budget.

**168. Combinatorial invariance of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.** Isomorphic Bruhat intervals in arbitrary Coxeter systems have identical equal-parameter KL polynomials — the full conjecture. *Approach:* induction on interval size with refined labeled combinatorial data. *Lesson:* enriched labels make poset-interval invariants inductive.

**169. Shareshian–Wachs e-positivity.** The chromatic quasisymmetric function of every natural unit interval graph is e-positive, with N[q]-coefficients counting explicit permutations. *Approach:* marked-clique expansion plus bijections to q-weighted permutation statistics. *Lesson:* positivity is strongest when coefficients count objects.

**170. Sharp logarithmic exponents for off-diagonal Ramsey numbers.** For fixed s ≥ 5, r(s,t) = t^{s−1}/(log t)^{s−2+o(1)}, matching the upper bound's log scale exactly. *Approach:* hypergraph containers above; randomness/alteration below. *Lesson:* when containers fix the log-exponent, only a tuned lower-bound construction remains.

**171. Hypercube Ramsey conjecture.** The two-color Ramsey number of Q_n is Θ(2^n) — linear in the cube's vertex count (Burr–Erdős). *Approach:* dependent-random-choice embedding into any dense color class; random-coloring lower bound. *Lesson:* small-separator targets admit linear Ramsey bounds.

**172. Classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations.** Configurations monochromatic at original scale in every coloring of high-dimensional space are characterized by an algebraic condition over the coordinate field; Leader–Russell–Walters disproved. *Approach:* Galois-theoretic criterion; explicit counterexample outside the transitive-set class. *Lesson:* Euclidean Ramsey behavior is algebraic, not metric.

**173. Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture.** Every finite oriented graph has a vertex with at least as many distance-two out-neighbors as out-neighbors. *Approach:* extremal-minimality analysis of the shortest-path structure. *Lesson:* long-open local statements can yield to one minimality idea.

**174. Deterministic strong thin spanning trees.** Every k-edge-connected multigraph has a spanning tree loading every cut by ≤ C/k, found deterministically in polynomial time even with binary-encoded multiplicities. *Approach:* derandomization of bounded-correlation random-tree distributions via conditional expectations. *Lesson:* random-tree existence proofs algorithmize without losing guarantees.

**175. Talagrand's conjectures; decomposition at expectation thresholds.** Integral and fractional expectation thresholds are within a universal constant; Talagrand's discrete-convexity conjecture holds; graph edges decompose into boundedly many pieces with thresholds within constants of the original (Ascoli–He–Park–Talagrand). *Approach:* fragment/cover duality — spread measures and minimum-fragment arguments with entropy-optimized covers. *Lesson:* fractional threshold relaxations are constant-factor faithful, the right abstraction for decomposition.

**176. The second Kahn–Kalai conjecture.** *(DEEP DIVE)* p_c(n,H) ≤ 2048·e^50·p_E(n,H)(1 + log_2 h) with explicit universal constants — a single logarithm in the edge count, sharper than the abstract Kahn–Kalai log in set size.

*Approach:* The obstacle: Park–Pham controls q(F_H), while p_E ≤ q(F_H) makes the abstract theorem useless here. Prior work reached q_f(F_H) = O(p_E·log(2e(H))) but kept an O(log²) loss against the true threshold. This paper directly bounds the fractional-cover cost of the copy family by p_E·(1+log_2 h), via rooted-extension decomposition accounting for multiplicities N(H,F) — containing H supplies *all* copies of each subgraph F — then applies fragment machinery at the one-half budget. *Explicitness:* explicit constants, deterministic proof, fully transparent. *Lesson:* the graph expectation threshold is the sharp input; tracking subgraph multiplicities recovers the single logarithm.

**177. Bounded-degree coboundary expanders in every dimension.** Arbitrarily large d-dimensional complexes (d ≥ 3) with bounded vertex degrees and uniform F_2 coboundary expansion below degree d. *Approach:* coset-complex product scaffolding with graded cosystolic expansion. *Lesson:* high-dimensional expansion survives bounded degree once agreeability is dimension-robust.

**178. Deterministic nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs in every fixed degree.** Simple d-regular nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs on every large even n, output explicitly (adjacency list) in polynomial bit time. *Approach:* interlacing-polynomial 2-lift technology with derandomized sign selection plus bipartiteness-breaking. *Lesson:* interlacing existence proofs become explicit by derandomizing sign choices.

**179. Circulant Hadamard conjecture.** Real circulant Hadamard matrices exist exactly in orders 1 and 4; perfect aperiodic-autocorrelation binary sequences only at lengths 2,3,4,5,7,11,13. *Approach:* cyclotomic-field factorization of the autocorrelation orthogonality equations. *Lesson:* algebraic number theory decides finite-exception autocorrelation problems.

**180. Barnette's Hamiltonian-cycle conjecture.** Every finite simple cubic bipartite planar 3-vertex-connected graph is Hamiltonian; equivalent Pfaffian three-edge-path formulation. *Approach:* paired-state/Pfaffian orientation duality organizing the case analysis. *Lesson:* planar-bipartite dualities tame Hamiltonicity case explosions.

**181. Erdős–Gallai cycle decomposition.** Edges of every n-vertex simple graph partition into ≤ Cn cycles and single edges, even dense. *Approach:* ear/DFS decomposition with charging bounding long-cycle splitting. *Lesson:* linear decomposition bounds need only a constant budget per vertex.

**182. Power savings for polynomial-difference-free sets.** For intersective h of degree k, h-difference-free subsets of [N] have size O_h(N^{1−c_k}); prime analogue with unit roots. *Approach:* Fourier transference with energy increments; intersectivity converts modulus obstructions into density increments. *Lesson:* intersectivity makes the increment loop terminate with power saving.

**183. Power savings for halving lines and k-sets.** O(n^{4/3−ε}) halving lines for sets with no three collinear; generally O(n(k+1)^{1/3−ε_0}) strictly separable k-sets. *Approach:* incidence/crossing cell decomposition refining Dey's method. *Lesson:* incidence improvements transfer directly down the k-set induction.

**184. Coloring and independence with forbidden subgraphs.** *(DEEP DIVE)* For fixed r ≥ 4, every K_r-free graph of large maximum degree Δ has correspondence chromatic number O_r(Δ/ log Δ) — the Alon–Krivelevich–Sudakov conjecture in stronger DP-coloring form; also the Ajtai–Erdős–Komlós–Szemerédi independence conjecture: K_r-free graphs of average degree d have independent sets of size Ω_r(n log d/d).

*Approach:* Per section 1.2: work on the cover graph H whose vertices are color entries and whose edges are the forbidden matching pairs; H is K_r-free with lists as independent sets, so correspondences are handled without identifying entries across lists. Random activation rounds are controlled by a three-term potential — relative entropy of weights, a linear weight-loss penalty, and incident edge mass D_v = Σ w_i w_j. Triangles create positive correlations (one neighbor activation kills two entries), costing ~twice the weighted triangle mass; Lemma 2.1's random multiplications/deletions offset it. The triangle-vs-edge inequality follows by LP duality against prices, dyadic grouping by median price, and entropy-guided vertex splitting; concentration under bounded per-list participation completes each round. *Explicitness:* randomized rounds with a potential proof — algorithmically constructive and transparent. *Lesson:* entropy compression with a triangle-aware potential is the natural home for Δ/ log Δ bounds; correspondence covers make it robust to list-coloring generality.

**185. Counterexamples to infinite matroid intersection and packing/covering.** The unrestricted infinite matroid intersection and packing/covering conjectures fail in ZFC via two self-dual partitional matroids on a countable set; Joó's question answered negatively. *Approach:* explicit set-theoretic self-dual construction; finitary restrictions save Nash-Williams' conjecture. *Lesson:* self-duality produces counterexamples to duality-flavored infinitary conjectures.

**186. Friedgut–Kalai threshold-width conjectures.** Monotone relabeling-invariant graph properties have threshold width O((log n)^{−2}); r-uniform hypergraph properties O_r((log n)^{−r/(r−1)}). *Approach:* sharpened Bourgain-type hypergraph influence bounds (valid nonmonotonically) fed into Russo analysis. *Lesson:* influence inequalities are the universal engine of threshold-width statements.

**187. Snaky in 21 Maker moves.** Maker can force the six-cell Snaky shape (up to isometry) within 21 of her own moves on the empty infinite square grid. *Approach:* explicit forcing strategy certified by finite game-tree case analysis. *Lesson:* small achievement games are finite verifiable strategy certificates.

**188. Sharp constant in random triangle removal.** Uniform random triangle removal from K_n leaves ~n^{3/2}/(2√2) edges with mean-square convergence (Joos–Kühn triangle case). *Approach:* Wormald-type ODE approximation pushed to second-order fluctuations. *Lesson:* sharp constants come from second-order ODE analysis.

**189. Exact cycle–clique Ramsey numbers.** R(C_m,K_n) = (m−1)(n−1)+1 for m ≥ n ≥ 3, except R(C_3,K_3)=6 (Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp). *Approach:* rigidity — classification of extremal colorings of the lower-bound construction plus 2-connected order bookkeeping. *Lesson:* exact off-diagonal Ramsey numbers hinge on rigidity of extremal colorings.

**190. Polynomial removal fails for ordered binary matrices.** A fixed 66×66 pattern requiring super-polynomially many entry changes (in ordered distance) for some high-density matrices. *Approach:* explicit probabilistic construction with an additive (Behrend-type) deletion barrier under order-preserving copies. *Lesson:* order shifts the removal obstruction from local to additive.

**191. Heilbronn triangle power improvement.** For all large n there exist n points in the unit square with every triangle of area ≥ n^{−2+c}, an absolute c > 0, disproving the conjectured n^{−2+o(1)} ceiling. *Approach:* randomized construction with deletion controlling small-area triangles by moment counts. *Lesson:* probabilistic constructions beat conjectured optima when obstruction counts are superlinear in moment bounds.

**192. Counterexample to the Gopalan–Servedio conjecture.** For every C there is a sign-valued Boolean f with Σ_i |f̂({i})| > C·deg(f), refuting the square-root degree bound on linear Fourier mass. *Approach:* explicit gadget stacking symmetric components whose linear coefficients add while degree stays bounded. *Lesson:* degree/mass conjectures fail by stacking gadgets with additive violations and subadditive degree.

### Cross-listed highlights (catalogued in adjacent subjects; assigned here)

**The Falconer distance conjecture in all dimensions.** *(DEEP DIVE)* For every d ≥ 2, compact E ⊂ R^d with dim_H E > d/2 has L^1(Δ(E)) > 0 — a positive-measure distance set at the strict threshold, with no regularity, Ahlfors, product, or Fourier-decay assumptions, and no endpoint claim.

*Approach:* Per the preprint: multiscale decomposition of irregular measures in the Keleti–Shmerkin tradition — dyadically regular pieces, scale-dependent directional tests, Lipschitz-profile accounting — but with a *two-depth potential* (Section 10) paying the recursion's costs where prior optimizations controlled only a sum of interval drops. Angular preparation gives a linear gain via two slicing regimes over an Orponen–Shmerkin incidence core; spherical stationary phase with global angular coefficients yields the shell estimates assembling the limiting distance measure. *Explicitness:* pure analysis, nonconstructive constants. *Lesson:* multiscale recursions need a potential with one depth layer per cost layer; the two-depth structure removes all prior dimensional offsets.

**The dyadic case of the Erdős similarity conjecture.** *(DEEP DIVE)* A compact subset of [0,1] of measure arbitrarily close to 1 containing no affine copy of {2^{−n}}, for every translation and every nonzero dilation.

*Approach:* Per the preprint: iterative interval construction as a "periodic hitting problem" — separated index windows on a tree protected by random routing (tables selecting cells, conditioning at centers, trials through a first default) so every candidate affine placement hits a deleted cell at an independently tested terminal scale; exceptional centers removed by union-bounded scheduling; the survivor is compact. *Explicitness:* constructive procedure with tunable measure 1−δ, but avoidance is probabilistic over infinitely many scales — not a machine-checkable certificate. *Lesson:* similarity avoidance reduces to scheduling independent hitting tests across scales; randomness in the construction, not the proof, does the work.

## Part II — Dynamical Systems and Ergodic Theory

**143. Uniform limit-cycle bounds (Hilbert's 16th problem).** Isolated periodic orbits of a planar polynomial vector field are bounded uniformly in degree; quintic Liénard systems have exactly two limit cycles. *Approach:* uniform abelian-integral bounds; exact Liénard analysis via divergence-integral monotonicity. *Lesson:* finiteness for ODE families follows from uniform abelian-integral bounds.

**144. Banach's simple Lebesgue-spectrum problem.** A smooth volume-preserving diffeomorphism of T^3 has simple Lebesgue spectrum on the entire complex mean-zero L^2. *Approach:* Anosov–Katok-style approximation limit upgraded to smoothness. *Lesson:* prescribed spectral simplicity survives smoothness requirements.

**145. Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem.** Every invertible mixing probability-preserving transformation is mixing of all finite orders. *Approach:* a rate-free criterion upgrading pairwise mixing to joining-rigidity of all finite orders. *Lesson:* rate-free arguments are the right generality; this is the bridge entry 154 needs.

**146. Sinai's positive-entropy conjecture for the standard map.** The standard sine map has positive metric entropy w.r.t. area for every sufficiently large parameter — a full parameter tail. *Approach:* perturbative invariant-cone/Lyapunov control on a positive-measure invariant set, retaining measure away from KAM islands. *Lesson:* entropy positivity for specific families needs family-specific invariant structures.

**147. Near-boundary Birkhoff conjecture.** Smooth strictly convex planar billiards of positive curvature are ellipses if a full grazing annulus is continuously foliated by invariant essential curves; a continuous caustic collar suffices too. *Approach:* the caustic foliation determines the generating function's phase dynamics; analytic continuation forces elliptic integrability. *Lesson:* boundary-detected integrability rigidifies the entire billiard.

**148. Dimension formula for self-similar measures on the line.** dim_H = min(1, h_RW/|λ|) for every finite-similarities IFS, no separation assumption — exact overlaps and unequal ratios allowed. *Approach:* Hochman-type microscale entropy/transversality arguments adapted to unequal contractions. *Lesson:* random-composition entropy is the dimension input; separation is removable.

**149. Permanence for weakly reversible reaction networks.** Every weakly reversible mass-action system has, in each positive stoichiometric class, a common compact convex forward-invariant absorbing set — uniform positive lower and finite upper concentration bounds. *Approach:* constructive monomial Lyapunov function whose level sets repel the class boundary. *Lesson:* permanence is a Lyapunov-barrier statement; weak reversibility supplies the barrier globally.

**150. Weak mixing of irrational triangular billiards.** The billiard flow of every nondegenerate triangle with an angle irrational relative to π is weakly mixing for area × uniform direction — no genericity or Diophantine restrictions. *Approach:* translation-surface unfolding; renormalization input extended to the full irrational class by continuity in angle data. *Lesson:* typical-surface spectral input extends to all irrational angles when the criterion is angle-continuous.

**151. A C^1 counterexample to Shub's entropy conjecture.** A noninvertible C^1 map of a compact manifold with zero topological entropy but eigenvalue 2 on H_2. *Approach:* explicit H_2-expanding algebraic model, C^1-perturbed to erase orbit complexity while keeping the homology map. *Lesson:* homological entropy rigidity requires invertibility/smoothness.

**152. Zero entropy does not guarantee a smooth positive-volume model.** A zero-entropy ergodic invertible transformation not measurably conjugate to any C^∞ diffeomorphism preserving a smooth positive density on any compact finite-dimensional manifold. *Approach:* a dimension-independent measure-theoretic invariant of smooth positive-measure-preserving systems, violated by the example. *Lesson:* smooth realization has measurable invariants beyond entropy.

**153. Arithmetic classification of Bernoulli convolutions.** Singularity vs absolute continuity of unbiased Bernoulli convolutions for every λ ∈ (0,1) is decided by an explicit one-sided approximation condition on finite sets of algebraic units; singular at reciprocals of every quartic Salem number in (1,2). *Approach:* transcendence analysis of overlap structure plus entry 148's entropy-dimension bookkeeping. *Lesson:* Bernoulli-convolution arithmetic is decidable from finite algebraic data.

**154. Pointwise multiple ergodic averages for mixing transformations.** *(DEEP DIVE)* For every invertible mixing T, every finite length n, and fixed bounded functions, the averages (1/N)Σ_k Π_j f_j(T^{jk}x) converge a.e. to the product of the integrals, along all N — on any probability space, with no mixing rate or standardness.

*Approach:* Per the preprint: extends the authors' fourfold mixing theorem using two companion results — all-orders mixing (entry 145) and pointwise triple/fourfold convergence for mixing systems. Induction on length: Host–Kra norm convergence is upgraded to pointwise by a Bourgain-style double-recurrence orthogonality argument whose error-term L^2 mass is killed by rate-free all-orders mixing — no rate or standardness needed; the null set depends on the fixed tuple. *Explicitness:* pure soft analysis, no certificates; the dependency is modular and explicit. *Lesson:* unconditional all-orders mixing lets Bourgain's orthogonality scheme iterate without rate hypotheses — two long-standing problems unlock each other.

## Meta-lessons (Combinatorics & Dynamics)

- **Probabilistic constructions with explicit scheduling dominate the counterexamples.** Heilbronn (191), van der Waerden bounds (160), ordered matrix removal (190), and the dyadic Erdős similarity case build objects by randomized procedures analyzed by union bounds and moments; the deliverable is existence with tuned parameters — generally *not* machine-checkable certificates.
- **Entropy, containers, and compression power the upper bounds.** Hadwiger (157) itself uses entropy minimizers, containers, capacitated cuts, and weak-regularity energy increments; correspondence coloring (184) is an entropy-compression potential; Ramsey off-diagonals (170) and expectation thresholds (175, 176) run on fragments and spread-measure duality. The second Kahn–Kalai theorem is the cleanest: explicit constant (2048·e^50), deterministic proof — a fully transparent certificate.
- **Rank/interpolation and algebraic-parity certificates turn topology into exact algebra.** The crossing-number solution (165) is a polynomial-interpolation rank bound over signed-crossing matrices — exact and checkable in principle. The Hadwiger hole relation is a parity identity (0 = 1 around a hole triangle) over F_2 moment matrices; its graphs are explicitly parametric (g = 10^9+1) and deterministic, but instance verification is infeasible (NP-hard parameters) and the proof's compactness arguments keep it short of machine-checkability.
- **Small explicit gadgets still decide finite open problems — the genuinely verifiable tier.** Sidorenko (35 vertices/66 edges), ordered matrix removal (66×66), Borsuk d = 9, Ryser prime-order hypergraphs, the Boolean-degree counterexample (192), and above all Snaky-in-21 (a finite verifiable forcing strategy) are concrete objects a referee or program can check.
- **Multiscale recursions need potentials matched to their cost layers.** Falconer (two-depth potential), higher-dimensional distinct distances (multi-degree Hilbert-function control), and intersective polynomial differences (multi-scale density increments) succeed because each recursion layer got its own accounting device.
- **In dynamics, rate-free structural theorems compound.** All-orders mixing (145) directly unlocks pointwise multiple averages (154); the separation-free dimension formula (148) powers the Bernoulli-convolution classification (153). The subjects interlock: the same entropy/influence toolkit (175, 186, 148) governs thresholds, self-similar dimensions, and Szemerédi-type transference (159).
