# Report: Differential Geometry (Subject 7) and Convex & Metric Geometry (Subject 8)

## Subject 7: Differential geometry (families 333–361)

**333. Smooth isometric immersions of surfaces into R^4.** Every closed smooth Riemannian surface — nonorientable, arbitrary curvature — admits a smooth isometric immersion into R^4. Codimension two gives the Gauss–Codazzi system slack that R^3 lacks. *Lesson:* one extra ambient dimension can make an overdetermined PDE solvable.

**334. Smooth surface metric with no local immersion in R^3.** A smooth metric on (-1,1)^2, Euclidean to all orders at the origin, admits no smooth local isometric immersion into R^3. Flat terms pin second-fundamental-form invariants whose Gauss-equation compatibility fails. *Lesson:* obstructions hide in infinitely flat data.

**335. ★ Gromov's integral scalar-curvature inequality.** ∫(Scal_g^-)^{n/2} dV ≥ a_n‖M‖ for all closed oriented n-manifolds, n ≥ 3, ‖M‖ the simplicial volume; positive scalar curvature forces rational inessentiality (Gromov–Lawson) and flatness on closed aspherical manifolds. *Approach* (`An-integral-scalar-curvature-bound-for-real-simplicial-volume`): conformal reduction to a bounded cocycle in probability form on boundary flags; measured equivariant graph families are deformed through sweeps of complete leaves where the positive scalar-curvature prescription localizes; a conformal bootstrap gives entropy increments with controlled surplus, coarea over measured bands bounds the descended degree, and the ℓ¹ mass of the fundamental cycle yields the bound. *Lesson:* sweep/degree bookkeeping turns analytic energy into bounded-cohomology rigidity.

**336. Spectral scalar curvature and codimension-two width.** A complete n-manifold with -4Δ + Scal ≥ 1 as a quadratic-form inequality maps continuously to a complex of dimension ≤ n-2 with uniformly bounded fibers, replacing the pointwise hypothesis in Gromov's Urysohn-width machinery. *Lesson:* spectral curvature formulations are more robust than pointwise ones.

**337. Cartan–Hadamard isoperimetry and CAT(0) fillings.** Sharp perimeter comparison with the equal-volume model ball in every complete simply connected manifold of curvature ≤ 0, all dimensions; equality forces round balls; sharp Euclidean filling bounds hold in proper CAT(0) spaces. p-Laplacian comparison functions bootstrap it. *Lesson:* isoperimetry and p-harmonic potential theory are two faces of one inequality.

**338. Yau's uniformization conjecture.** Every complete noncompact Kähler manifold with strictly positive holomorphic bisectional curvature is biholomorphic to C^n, all dimensions. Parabolic (Ricci-flow-type) deformation plus a structure theory extracting an affine-algebraic model from positivity. *Lesson:* curvature positivity plus completeness substitutes for compactness.

**339. Katok's entropy rigidity conjecture.** In dimension ≥ 3 with strictly negative sectional curvature, normalized Liouville measure maximizes geodesic-flow entropy iff the metric is locally symmetric. Entropy comparisons via the Bowen–Margulis measure become curvature integrals. *Lesson:* entropy functionals are curvature detectors.

**340. Counterexample to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture.** For large even N, T*(S^9 × S^{N-1}) contains a closed exact Lagrangian diffeomorphic to the base but not Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section — Floer-theoretic separation in a high-dimensional product. *Lesson:* long-tested conjectures can fail only in a growing-parameter regime.

**341. Donaldson's hypersymplectic deformation conjecture.** Any positive triple of closed two-forms on a closed oriented four-manifold, after constant normalization, deforms through positive triples to a hyperkähler triple with cohomology fixed. A geometric flow converges to the parallel configuration. *Lesson:* flows on positivity-constrained spaces reach the integrable point.

**342. Donaldson's tamed-to-compatible conjecture.** Every smooth almost complex structure on a closed four-manifold tamed by a symplectic form admits a compatible one. Variational: maximize a functional over the tame cone; taming gives coercivity. *Lesson:* tamed/compatible gaps close by optimizing over the dual cone.

**343. Sharp symplectic ball-packing in higher dimensions.** For 2n ≥ 6, closed symplectic balls of capacities R_i embed disjointly in a ball of capacity R iff ∑R_i^n < R^n and R_i + R_j < R pairwise (Siegel–Yao). Capacity propagation and Fourier restriction show the only non-volume obstructions are pairwise. *Lesson:* packing obstructions collapse to pairwise checks plus volume.

**344. The metric Blaschke conjecture.** A closed Riemannian manifold with inj = diam is, up to scaling, a compact rank-one symmetric space. The equality forces the cut-locus structure; integral-geometry identities identify the CROSS. *Lesson:* one global metric identity determines the model via cut-locus integral geometry.

**345. Infinitely many closed geodesics on spheres and three-manifolds.** Every metric on S^n (n ≥ 2) has infinitely many prime closed geodesics with distinct images; same for finite spherical covers and all closed three-manifolds, without nondegeneracy. Minimax classes with distinct extrema; degeneracy handled via covers. *Lesson:* degeneracy is no obstruction when minimax classes separate.

**346. Sharp singular-set bounds for stationary integral varifolds.** The singular set of any stationary integral m-varifold has Hausdorff dimension ≤ m-1 (sharp), and zero m-measure on round spheres. Quantitative stratification plus constancy for tangent measures. *Lesson:* quantitative stratification turns energy monotonicity into singular-set dimension bounds.

**347. Counterexamples to strong Arnold fixed-point bounds.** On Kähler manifolds of real dimension 22 the deficit below the stable Morse number is unbounded; a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the quadric threefold has exactly three fixed points (functions need four). Floer complexes realize minimal counts. *Lesson:* homological bounds and actual counts separate under orchestrated degeneracy.

**348. Nonnegatively curved Einstein four-manifolds and an L² gap.** Closed Einstein four-manifolds with positive Einstein constant and Sec ≥ 0 have universal covers S^4, CP², or S²×S²; any closed simply connected Sec ≥ 0 four-manifold with small scale-invariant L² trace-free Ricci is diffeomorphic to one of these. Zero-plane rigidity classifies equality cases; compactness toward the list turns L² smallness into diffeomorphism type. *Lesson:* classification + L² gap = stability theorem.

**349. The Solomon–Yau least-volume conjecture.** A closed connected minimal immersion in S^{m+1} (m ≥ 2) with non-totally-geodesic image has volume at least the smallest minimal Clifford product, counting multiplicity. Jacobi-operator eigenvalue comparison against Clifford families. *Lesson:* spectral comparison against model families turns variational minimization into algebra.

**350. Yau's nodal conjecture: surfaces yes, higher dimensions no.** Nodal length on any fixed smooth closed surface is O(√λ); but fixed smooth metrics in dimensions three and four (S^3 arbitrarily close to round) violate the upper bound, and in dimension five nodal measure beats λ^{1/2+ε₀}. Upper bound: microlocal analysis plus GMT of the zero set; counterexamples: eigenfunction packets concentrating nodal mass. *Lesson:* the surface cancellation mechanism fails from dimension three up.

**351. ★ Scalar curvature detects four-dimensional Ricci-flow singularities.** A smooth Ricci flow on a closed four-manifold extends past any finite time with bounded scalar curvature; in higher dimensions bounded scalar curvature no longer prevents unbounded full curvature at the singular time. *Approach* (`Bounded-scalar-curvature-and-smooth-extension…`): contradiction via a scale-invariant "static functional" monotone along the flow. Assuming failure at T: heat localization isolates a small bad set; quotient cones, quantitative necks, and bubble trees define a root scale; concentration estimates exploit the 4D curvature decomposition (the spherical projection captures the missing radial mode) to control the exterior collar. A generalized-inverse gauge construction extends the metric across a finite annulus; the static inequality on each time patch forces the root geometry trivial, contradicting the lower bound. *Lesson:* monotone functionals plus bubble-tree compactness yield flow continuation, dimension by dimension.

**352. Finite-time singularity of Calabi flow.** A smooth U(10)-invariant Fubini–Study metric on CP^10 develops unbounded scalar curvature in finite Calabi-flow time, disproving Chen's long-time existence conjecture even in a class containing a cscK metric. Symmetry reduction collapses the flow to a parabolic ODE system. *Lesson:* invariant ansätze reduce flows to ODEs — the cheapest route to long-existence questions either way.

**353. Sharp dimension threshold for affine Bernstein rigidity.** Complete locally uniformly convex affine-maximal graphs in dimensions 3–9 are elliptic paraboloids; a smooth nonquadratic entire example in dimension ten is sharp. Fourth-order Monge–Ampère-type estimates with a Simons-style exponent threshold at n = 10. *Lesson:* Bernstein rigidity holds below an explicit exponent threshold.

**354. Isoperimetric regions in the cubic three-torus.** Full classification in the unit flat three-torus: balls, tubes about shortest geodesics, coordinate slabs, and complements, with transition volumes 4π/81 and 1/π. GMT regularity restricts candidates to CMC surfaces with lattice symmetry; direct comparison finishes. *Lesson:* symmetry + regularity reduces global variational problems to finite phase diagrams.

**355. Unique tangent flows at the first surface singularity.** At the first singular time of a smooth compact embedded surface in R^3, all fixed-center backward tangent flows agree as area measures at every negative time in ambient coordinates — no mean-convexity or prescribed model. Finite-jet blow-up analysis forces a multiplicity-one self-shrinker. *Lesson:* the first singularity is analytically rigid.

**356. Gigli's characterization of Alexandrov curvature.** A space is an n-dimensional Alexandrov space of curvature ≥ κ iff, with H^n, it is a full-support RCD((n-1)κ,n) space with distributional sectional curvature ≥ κ in Gigli's test classes. RCD heat-flow/Bochner machinery plus weak Hessian bounds along geodesics equate triangle comparison with the distributional calculus. *Lesson:* the synthetic curvature hierarchy collapses when distributional operators are allowed.

**357. ★ Bi-Lipschitz coordinates at every regular RCD point.** Every regular point (all pointed GH tangents ≅ R^n) of a noncollapsed RCD(K,n) space has an open neighborhood bi-Lipschitz to an open subset of R^n with constant depending only on n — resolving the regular-point bi-Lipschitz conjecture and giving a countable bi-Lipschitz atlas on a full-measure open region. *Approach* (`Bi-Lipschitz-Coordinates-at-Regular-Points…`): upgrade Cheeger–Colding bi-Hölder charts to bi-Lipschitz by controlling all point pairs across infinitely many scales, not just distances to a center. Noncollapsed RCD tools (De Philippis–Gigli volume rigidity, quantitative Euclidean approximation) supply scale-independent flatness at a regular point, and a multiscale induction keeps ambient and chart distances uniformly comparable — no injectivity-radius or Morrey L² hypotheses needed. *Lesson:* tangent-uniqueness plus multiscale control turns synthetic Ricci bounds into manifold coordinates.

**358. No-conjugate-points metric without any K ≤ 0 metric.** A closed orientable three-manifold admits a metric without conjugate points but no metric of nonpositive sectional curvature. A topological obstruction to K ≤ 0, paired with a construction of the geodesic flow. *Lesson:* "no conjugate points" is strictly weaker than K ≤ 0 already in dimension three.

**359. Negative Kähler curvature without bounded holomorphic coordinates.** A contractible domain in C^3 carries a complete negatively pinched Kähler metric but no bounded holomorphic coordinates; a higher-dimensional example with sectional curvature ≤ -1 has only constant bounded holomorphic functions. Explicit construction plus potential-theoretic obstructions. *Lesson:* negative pinching controls geodesics, not the function algebra.

**360. Villani's convexity conjecture and regular optimal transport.** Weak Ma–Trudinger–Wang curvature implies convexity of every tangent injectivity domain, and squared-distance optimal maps between bounded-away densities are Hölder with Hölder inverse. A supporting-mountain argument: subgradients of the squared-distance potential are minimizing velocities with a global supporting function. *Lesson:* transport regularity reduces to injectivity-domain convexity.

**361. Counterexamples to Yau's harmonic dimension bound.** For arbitrarily large k, complete metrics on R^3 (and higher even dimensions) with Ric ≥ 0 admit ≥ (k+2)² harmonic functions of growth ≤ k, exceeding the Euclidean (k+1)². The metrics are Euclidean near the origin with nonunique tangent cones at infinity. *Lesson:* harmonic-function counts see geometry at infinity.

## Subject 8: Convex and metric geometry (families 087–101)

**087. ★ The Mahler conjectures and symplectic width.** Both Mahler conjectures hold in every dimension: |K||K°| ≥ (n+1)^{n+1}/(n!)² for general bodies, equality exactly at simplices; ≥ 4^n/n! for origin-symmetric bodies, equality exactly at Hanner polytopes (all equality cases classified); sharp functional Mahler inequalities follow, and symmetric polar products have Gromov width 4. *Approach* (`The-Mahler-Conjecture-for-General-Convex-Bodies`, `The-symmetric-Mahler-conjecture…`): Klartag's cone/Laplace correspondence reduces the volume product to a Laplace-transform product χ_C(V)χ_D(U) ≥ 1 on dual cones. Injective smoothed maps into the cones are built by Moreau projections of a Gaussian Z = Σ^{1/2}G, with covariance and biases chosen so Jacobians plus Jensen give the log-Laplace lower bound. The derivative matrices P_z neither commute nor increase in z; the proof compares them against spectral thresholds of a linear Gaussian matrix (Hermite expansion, matrix divided differences, a strict interval inequality); equality forces commutation — the cone splits into half-lines, a simplex. Final inequalities carry finite rational certificates. *Lesson:* polar duality becomes probabilistic transportation into cones; equality reduces to commutation — machine-certifiable at the last step.

**088. Petty's projection-volume conjecture and simplex counterexamples.** Ellipsoids uniquely minimize projection-body volume for n ≥ 4, and the full Lutwak–Petty projection inequalities hold; yet products of simplices beat Brannen's proposed 20-dimensional simplex maximum by an exponential factor. Support-function calculus identifies ellipsoids as the unique variational minimum. *Lesson:* "simplex-maximal" patterns die under products.

**089. Bounded-distortion L₁ embeddings of planar and bounded-treewidth graphs.** Shortest-path metrics of planar graphs embed into L₁ with universal distortion, and bounded-treewidth graphs with treewidth-only distortion (Gupta–Newman–Rabinovich–Sinclair), with uniformly bounded flow–cut gaps. L₁ = nonnegative cut combinations; the proof samples separators preserving local separation laws. *Lesson:* L₁ embeddability is cut-sampling; topology controls distortion and yields flow–cut gaps free.

**090. ★ Universal optimality of the triangular lattice.** The triangular lattice minimizes lower-limit energy per particle among all locally finite planar configurations of centered-disk density one, for every nonnegative completely monotone potential of squared distance (Gaussians, inverse powers, infinite energies allowed); it also minimizes renormalized Riesz energies for 0 < s < 2 and Coulomb energy, resolving Sandier–Serfaty and Brauchart–Hardin–Saff. *Approach* (`Universal-optimality-of-the-triangular-lattice`, `An-atomic-certificate…`): the Gaussian route — sharp Fourier lower bounds (auxiliary functions from Gaussian cardinal functions under coupled interpolation conditions) are proved for every Gaussian, and Bernstein's theorem writes any completely monotone potential as a mixture of Gaussians, transferring the bound to the full class; heat-kernel comparison handles logarithmic and renormalized Riesz (jellium) energies. The competitor class is all density-one configurations, not just lattices — extending Montgomery's theta theorem via the atomic interpolation certificate. *Lesson:* Fourier-interpolation minorants — the E₈/Leech engine — transfer to the plane once the certificate is atomic.

**091. ★ The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture.** For origin-symmetric convex bodies in every dimension, |W[h_K^{1-λ}h_L^{1-λ}]| ≥ |K|^λ|L|^{1-λ}; hence the symmetric L_p Brunn–Minkowski inequality for 0 < p < 1 and, via Saroglou's transfer, the scalar-dilation B-conjecture for all even log-concave Radon measures. *Approach* (`The-logarithmic-Brunn-Minkowski-conjecture`): a symmetric polytope's indicator is approximated by exp(−|x|²/2 − ε∑(⟨p_i,x⟩/h_i)^q), reducing log-concavity under geometric slab-width changes to a variance bound for sums of even powers of linear forms. In moment coordinates the measure is the image of e^{−φ(z)}dz under x = ∇φ(z), with φ (Berman–Berndtsson, Klartag) of bounded positive Hessian; an operator tied to the moment Hessian generates a dense class of even test functions, and a tensor calculation with explicit-square remainder plus two Cauchy–Schwarz steps yields the variance bound — a strengthened Brascamp–Lieb inequality. *Lesson:* local concavity in moment coordinates integrates to the global geometric inequality.

**092. Optimal order of convex-body covering density.** Worst-case translative covering density is Θ(n log n): every convex body has a lattice covering of density ≤ Cn log n, and symmetric bodies require ≥ cn log n even unrestrictedly. A Rogers-type lattice construction with logarithmic refinement; a volume-ratio lower bound. *Lesson:* lattice restriction costs at most logarithmic slack.

**093. Dimension-free log-Sobolev for subgaussian log-concave measures.** Centered log-concave densities with uniformly subgaussian linear marginals satisfy Ent_μ(f²) ≤ Ca²∫|Df|²dμ with a universal constant. The marginal hypothesis propagates to all one-dimensional marginals; the 1D inequality tensorizes. *Lesson:* controlling linear marginals is a dimension-free substitute for isotropicity.

**094. Subpolynomial dimension reduction in L_p.** Every n-point subset of L_p (1 < p < ∞) embeds into ℓ_p^d with distortion D and d = n^{o(1)} nonlinearly, answering Naor's question for p ≤ 2; exact embeddings need Θ(n²) dimension for p ≥ 2. p-stable probabilistic sampling; a metric-type lower bound for the exact regime. *Lesson:* dropping linearity buys an exponential dimension saving.

**095. Hyperbolicity cones without semidefinite lifts.** Some hyperbolicity cones admit no finite affine semidefinite lift with any number of auxiliary variables — disproving the Projected Lax and generalized Lax conjectures. Lift-obstruction theory (rank bounds, boundary rigidity of spectrahedral shadows) applied to a cone whose boundary violates every lift invariant. *Lesson:* conic-optimization expressivity has provable limits: hyperbolic programs beat SDPs.

**096. The Gaussian propeller conjecture.** The sum of squared Gaussian first moments of any finite measurable partition is ≤ 9/(8π), attained by three 2π/3 planar sectors extended orthogonally; with the Unique Games theorem, beating the (8/9)(1-1/k) loss for identity-target kernel clustering (fixed k ≥ 3) is NP-hard. Variational analysis of Gaussian moment functionals plus a deterministic UGC reduction. *Lesson:* continuous Gaussian partition problems encode discrete hardness — the constant doubles as the optimal approximation ratio.

**097. The Euclidean Steinitz–Bergström conjecture.** Every prescribed-order sequence in the Euclidean unit ball of R^d has a signing with all signed prefixes ≤ C√d (length-free), and zero-sum families reorder with the same bound; S₂(d) = Θ(√d). Discrepancy signing plus a zero-sum reordering lemma; matching lower bound. *Lesson:* signings plus reordering kill length dependence at the sharp √d.

**098. Negative answer to Lang–Plaut.** Every infinite-dimensional real Banach space contains a compact doubling set that embeds into no finite-dimensional normed space — even in Hilbert space. A doubling construction with a finite-dimensional embedding obstruction from metric type/cotype invariants. *Lesson:* doubling does not imply finite-dimensional embeddability.

**099. Sharp distortion of edit distance into ℓ₁.** The least ℓ₁-distortion of edit distance on words of length ≤ d is Θ(√(log d · log log d)), uniform over alphabets of size ≥ 2 — binary suffices below. Two independent tree constructions match the companion upper embedding. *Lesson:* string-metric lower bounds come from hierarchical tree-like obstructions.

**100. Counterexample to Bang's cylinder-covering bound.** The regular tetrahedron admits a finite cylinder covering whose total base area is below half its minimum orthogonal projection area — refuting Bang's half-area bound and the directionwise normalized conjecture in dimension three. Explicit construction, propagated to every nondegenerate tetrahedron by affine invariance. *Lesson:* plank-type "half the projection" bounds fail already for tetrahedra.

**101. The sharp simplex conjecture for isotropic constants.** Simples uniquely maximize the isotropic constant among convex bodies in every dimension (strong isotropic constant conjecture), and h(f) ≥ m + ½log det Cov(f) for log-concave densities, with equality exactly at affine images of products of one-sided exponentials. Entropic duality: the entropy bound is dual to the isotropic-constant bound, via localization/stability arguments. *Lesson:* information-theoretic and geometric constants are dual faces of one problem.

## Meta-lessons (Differential & Convex Geometry)

- **Certificate-based, machine-checkable inequalities are a core proof technology.** The Mahler proof closes its final scalar inequalities with finite rational certificates; triangular-lattice universal optimality uses an atomic interpolation certificate for sharp Gaussian Fourier minorants; and the planar Cohn–Elkies circle-packing paper (`A-sharp-Fourier-certificate-for-planar-circle-packing`) builds a radial Schwartz certificate attaining the optimal density π/(2√3), its global sign conditions verified by rigorous interval arithmetic, Taylor quotients, and Bernstein coefficient certificates, with an independent second rational check — also recovering uniqueness of the triangular packing among periodic equality cases. The object of proof is an explicit verifiable function.
- **Fourier duality is the master tool of extremal geometry.** Gaussian minorants plus mixtures (090), Fourier restriction for symplectic capacities (343), the two-point Cohn–Elkies bound (circle packing), and moment-map/Hessian tensor estimates (091) all convert geometric inequalities into positivity of an explicit transform; equality cases fall out as the certification's fixed points.
- **Flows and monotone functionals decide existence, rigidity, and singularity at once.** Ricci-flow extension from bounded scalar curvature (351) rests on a static monotone functional plus bubble-tree compactness; Calabi-flow blow-up (352) is a symmetric-reduction counterexample; hypersymplectic→hyperkähler deformation (341) is a flow to the integrable point.
- **Stability from classification: prove the equality case, then add an L² gap.** The Einstein chain (348) is the template — zero-plane rigidity classifies equality cases, L² smallness of trace-free Ricci upgrades to diffeomorphism type via compactness toward the list. The same degeneration logic appears in first-singular-time tangent-flow uniqueness (355) and bi-Lipschitz regularity of RCD spaces (357).
- **Isoperimetric and duality arguments transfer between worlds.** Cartan–Hadamard isoperimetry via p-harmonic potentials (337), torus isoperimetry as a finite phase diagram (354), Mahler duality through cone/Laplace transport (087), and the entropy↔isotropic-constant duality (101) exploit that extremal problems have dual formulations with coinciding equality cases; the transport/MTW chain (360) repeats this.
- **Results with direct computational/optimization content.** Mahler and simplex-isotropic bounds (087, 101) as verifiable volume-product and condition-number certificates; hyperbolicity cones without SDP lifts (095) delimiting solver expressivity; the Gaussian propeller constant 9/(8π) plus the Unique Games theorem (096) making kernel-clustering approximation hardness effective; the length-free C√d vector-balancing guarantee (097); planar-graph L₁ embeddings (089) giving bounded flow–cut gaps; and the interval-arithmetic circle-packing certificate as a reproducible computational artifact.
